Lab 0: Lab Checkoff Process and Expectations

This lab covers the following course topics:

  • Understanding how lab checkoffs work and what is required to receive lab credit.
  • Knowing where and when checkoffs are allowed (and not allowed).
  • Preparing to explain your submitted work to an Instructional Assistant (IA).
  • Maintaining your personal record of successful checkoffs using the checkoff receipt email.

Lab Description

Lab 0 is a process-focused lab. The purpose is to ensure you understand exactly how lab checkoffs work in this course, when you can get checked off, and what is required for your lab submission to count. Lab checkoffs are required for every lab in this course.

In this course, you will submit labs through Canvas like normal. However, submitting a lab is not enough. You must also attend an IA checkoff session and demonstrate understanding of the code you submitted. This lab explains the rules and expectations for that process.

Part 1: What a Lab Checkoff Is

A lab checkoff is a short 1-on-1 conversation with an Instructional Assistant (IA) where you explain your lab solution. The IA will ask you questions about your code and your implementation choices. The goal is to verify:

  • You completed the work yourself.
  • You understand what you submitted.
  • You can explain key parts of your solution in a reasonable way.
This is not a debugging session and it is not a guarantee that your code is fully correct. Your lab grade still depends on the usual correctness and requirements of the lab assignment.

Part 2: Where and When Checkoffs Are Allowed

Checkoffs can only happen during IA office hours. No checkoffs are allowed during lab time or in class. You are responsible for planning ahead so you can attend office hours before your checkoff window closes.

Part 3: Time Window and Resubmission Rules

When a lab assignment is assigned, you must:

  • Submit the lab first, and then
  • Attend an IA office hour checkoff to explain your submission.
You have two weeks from the date the lab is assigned in your section’s lab time to complete the checkoff. Failure to meet with an IA within the time constraints will result in you receiving a zero for the lab.

If you resubmit a lab assignment, you must be checked off again. Only the most recent submission before the most recent checkoff will be accepted.

If you do not demonstrate understanding during a checkoff, you may attempt again in the same way as before, but not on the same calendar day as another check for the same lab. You may request as many checks as you want within the two-week window, as long as:

  • There is no more than one checkoff per lab assignment per day.

Due to these constraints, we highly encourage you to meet with the IAs early to demonstrate understanding. IAs may be busy with personal matters, attending to other students, or generally unavailable close to the end of the two weeks.

Part 4: Understanding Ratings and Grade Impact

During a checkoff, the IA will record an understanding rating based on your explanation. If you receive a rating of "Developing Understanding", your lab grade will be halved from what your score would otherwise be.

Part 5: Your Checkoff Receipt Email (Required Record)

After a checkoff is completed, you will receive an email receipt that documents the checkoff. You are responsible for keeping this receipt as your record. You must have a successful checkoff receipt for each and every lab. Failure to have a record is on you.

Below is an example of the checkoff receipt email you will receive after an IA submits your checkoff form:

Subject Line: TA Checkoff Form - Lab Checkoff Receipt

Your form TA Checkoff Form has a new entry. Here are all the answers.

Lab: Lab 0: Sample Checkoff Lab

Current Understanding: Developing Understanding

Comments: Anything you might need to fix

Date: Tue, Jan 6, 2026 2:39 PM

Part 6: What You Should Prepare Before Office Hours

Before attending IA office hours for a checkoff, you should be able to:

  • Show the most recent version of your submitted code.
  • Explain how your program works at a high level.
  • Walk through key functions, loops, and data structures you used.
  • Explain how you tested your solution (and what you did when something failed).
  • Answer basic “why” questions about your implementation choices.
If you wait until the end of the two-week window, you may have limited opportunities to re-check if needed. Plan accordingly.
